    How to read it

    Low

    Nothing in the forecast that usually slows an airport down.

    Elevated

    Weather that often causes delays: gusty wind, snow, fog or heavy rain.

    High

    Weather that regularly stops or sharply cuts flights: thunderstorms, freezing rain, heavy snow, dense fog or severe gusts.

    How the risk level is worked out, and what it cannot tell you

    For every hour you are at an airport (from an hour before to an hour after each take-off or landing) Gatecast scores the forecast: thunderstorms, freezing rain, snowfall rate, visibility and fog, wind gusts and rain rate. The worst hour sets the airport's level. For a connection, a short layover raises the level and a long one lowers it, and bad weather where your inbound flight departs counts too, because late departures arrive late.

    This is a weather-based estimate, not a prediction from airline or air-traffic data. Airports differ: the same snowfall that closes one airport is routine at another. Flights are also delayed for reasons that have nothing to do with weather. Forecasts more than about a week out are an outlook only. Always check with your airline.
