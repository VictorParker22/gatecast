# Moderation policy: "Caught on camera"

Status: draft for review. Applies to every video shown in the "Caught on camera" section (police body cameras, dashcams, doorbell cameras, traffic and security cameras), on every page and at every box size.

## Why this section needs its own rule

These videos show real, identifiable people, often on the worst day of their life, and usually before any court has decided what happened. They draw attention, which is why they are on the page, and that is exactly why they can do harm: to the people shown, to viewers, and to the site (defamation claims, platform strikes, advertisers leaving). The rule below decides what runs. View counts only rank what has already passed.

## Principles

1. **Public interest, not shock.** A video runs because it shows something people have a reason to see: how an incident unfolded, how officials acted, a hazard others should know about. "It is violent" is never the reason.
2. **Accused is not guilty.** Nobody is described as having committed a crime unless they have been convicted.
3. **Minimise harm.** If a video can be told just as well without a face, a name, or the worst seconds, leave them out by choosing a different video. We do not edit other people's footage.
4. **Embed, never re-host.** We show the original publisher's player. If they take it down, it disappears from our page too.
5. **A person decides.** Automated checks only remove candidates. Nothing is published without a human reviewer approving it.

## Never shown

A video is rejected if it contains any of these, even briefly, even blurred by the source:

| # | Not allowed | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | Death or the moment of death | Fatal shootings, fatal crashes, bodies |
| N2 | Graphic injury | Visible serious wounds, blood, resuscitation, people in severe pain |
| N3 | Sexual violence or nudity | Any sexual offence, strip searches, people undressed |
| N4 | Identifiable children | Anyone who appears to be under 18 as a victim, suspect or witness, including their voice and their name |
| N5 | Suicide, self-harm or a mental-health crisis | Welfare checks, people in crisis, overdose responses |
| N6 | Victims of domestic abuse, sexual offences or trafficking | Whether or not they are named |
| N7 | Inside a private home or medical setting, where the people shown are not the subject of a charge | Home searches with family present, hospital rooms, ambulances |
| N8 | Footage we cannot verify | No identifiable original publisher, no date, no place |
| N9 | Footage published to humiliate | Compilations, "instant karma" edits, added music or sound effects, mocking captions |
| N10 | Footage that is sealed, under a court order, or released in breach of a publication ban | |
| N11 | Anything the original platform has age-restricted or removed | Checked at review and re-checked daily |
| N12 | Bystanders' faces, licence plates or home addresses as the focus | Unless the source has already blurred them |

## Allowed with conditions

| # | Allowed | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Arrests and police stops without graphic injury | Outcome line required (see "Every video"). No ridicule. |
| C2 | Use of force without graphic injury | Must come from the agency's official release or a named news organisation. Carries a "Shows use of force" label. Never the lead (No. 1) position. No ads next to it. |
| C3 | Crashes and near-misses with no visible injury | The source must state that nobody was seriously hurt, or the video shows people walking away. |
| C4 | Doorbell and security camera clips | Only from the owner's own public post or a licensed agency. Never taken from neighbourhood apps or private groups. Faces of passers-by who are not the subject must not be the focus. |
| C5 | Rescues and good-news clips | Encouraged. Aim for at least one in every five items. |

## Every video must have

- **A verified source.** The official channel of the agency that released it, a named news organisation, or a licensed video agency. The source name is shown and linked.
- **A date and a place.** If we do not know when and where, it does not run.
- **A neutral headline.** It says what the video shows. It does not say who is guilty, does not use words like "thug", "criminal", "Karen" or "instant karma", and does not promise violence ("you won't believe…", "brutal").
- **Careful wording about people.** "Police say", "is accused of", "was charged with". Names of people who are only suspected are left out unless a charge has been filed and reported by a named source.
- **An outcome line** when one is known: charges filed, dropped, acquittal, conviction, agency finding. If it changes, we update it.
- **A safe thumbnail.** No weapon pointed at a person, no blood, no child, no one on the ground being restrained.
- **A note if the footage was edited by the agency.** Many official releases are cut, narrated or captioned by the department; we say so.
- **A review stamp.** The page shows "Reviewed" and, where relevant, "Shows use of force".

## Ranking

"Most viewed" ranks approved videos only. View counts come from the original platform's own figures, shown as that platform reports them and never estimated. A video's rank can never override a rejection, and a reviewer may hold any video out of the top position.

## How review works

1. **Collect.** An automated job gathers candidates from an approved list of channels and feeds. It does not search the open web.
2. **Filter.** Candidates are dropped automatically if the platform has age-restricted them, if the title or description matches the "never shown" terms, or if the source is not on the approved list.
3. **Review.** A trained person watches the whole video, start to finish, against this policy and records approve or reject with the rule number.
4. **Second look.** Anything under C2, and anything a reviewer is unsure about, needs a second reviewer. If they disagree, it does not run.
5. **Publish** with headline, source, date, place, outcome line and labels.
6. **Re-check.** Every published video is re-checked daily for removal or age-restriction at the source and for a changed outcome. Videos leave the section after 14 days.

Every decision is logged: who reviewed, when, which rule, and what was shown.

## Requests to remove a video

- Anyone shown in a video, or their family or lawyer, can ask for it to be removed through a link on every video. We take it down while we look at the request and answer within two working days.
- We remove on request when: charges were dropped or the person was acquitted; the person is a victim, a witness or a bystander; the person was under 18; or the request shows a real risk to someone's safety.
- There is one route for every kind of request: privacy, copyright, a legal notice, or a request to erase personal data. Each gets a reference number, a decision with the reason, and a way to appeal.
- Copyright complaints are handled under the standard notice-and-takedown process, with a named contact.
- Corrections are made in place and marked as corrected.

## Advertising and audience

No advertising appears next to C2 videos. The section as a whole is excluded from ad categories that bar crime or violence content. The section is not aimed at children and is never placed on a page that is.

## Limits of this draft

This policy was written from published platform and journalism standards (YouTube's graphic-content and advertiser rules, the SPJ and RTDNA ethics codes) as summarised in `docs/research/camera_sources_moderation.md`. The legal parts (defamation wording, state rules on releasing body-camera video, privacy law outside the US) need review by a media lawyer before launch.

## Outside the United States

The section is shown only to visitors in countries where a named person has confirmed it meets local privacy and broadcasting rules. Until that is done for a country, visitors there see the other sections instead.

## Who owns this

A named editor owns this policy, approves the source list, and reviews a sample of decisions every week. The policy is reviewed every six months and after any complaint that leads to a removal.

## Measures

- Share of candidates rejected, by rule number.
- Removal requests received, and time to respond.
- Videos taken down after publishing, and why.
- Share of items that are rescues or good news.
