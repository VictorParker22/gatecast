# Join the forecast to the itinerary

The killer site is a free, no-login web page where a traveler enters origin, connecting hub, destination and flight times, and gets one plain-language weather-risk verdict for the trip, the reason behind it, and the hourly detail on demand. No mainstream product found in this research does that join: flight trackers treat weather as a map layer, weather products ignore the itinerary, and the FAA's own status page says it "is not flight-specific" ([FAA](https://www.fly.faa.gov/flyfaa/plain_upper.jsp)). The closest rival, Flighty, is iOS-only and paid, and its connection rating is built on minimum connection time, not weather ([Flighty help](https://flighty.com/help/connection-assistant); [Going.com](https://www.going.com/guides/flighty-review)). The data constraint shapes the product: live checks on 15 November 2026 found that only Open-Meteo forecasts and US National Weather Service alerts can be called directly from a browser without a key, while METAR, FAA delay programs and aircraft positions all need a small server-side proxy, and no keyless source of flight schedules or status exists at all. So the honest version one is a user-entered itinerary with a weather-risk verdict, clearly labelled as weather risk and not a delay prediction. Display research points the same way: lead with a verdict, put a number beside every icon, use three labelled risk levels, and show each airport in its own local time. One caveat applies throughout: the underlying notes come from search-result extracts, not full page reads, so prices and several statistics need re-checking before they are published.

## Travelers are told too little, too late, about airports they never check

Weather is the largest single cause of air-traffic delay in the US, but the headline figure depends on the denominator. The FAA attributes **74.26% of system-impacting delays over 15 minutes** (June 2017 to May 2023) to weather ([FAA Weather Delay FAQ](https://www.faa.gov/nextgen/programs/weather/faq)). The Weather Company puts weather at **25.4% of total US delay minutes in 2022 and 38.7% in 2019** ([The Weather Company](https://weathercompany.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/The-Weather-Company.The-impact-of-weather-on-U.S.-aviation-Data-in-action.INFOGRAPHIC_US-Downloadable_Revised-4-2.pdf)). The narrow "extreme weather" bucket airlines report is typically only 5-10% of delayed minutes, because most weather impact flows through the airspace-system category ([ai-analytics.org](https://ai-analytics.org/writing/bts-airline-performance/)). All three are correct; a consumer site that quotes one must say which.

The mechanism matters more than the number. When weather cuts an airport's arrival rate, flights bound for it are held at their departure point ([FAA](https://www.fly.faa.gov/ais/Help/ais_message_help.html)). A traveler in sunshine at the origin is delayed by fog at the hub, which is the link a general weather app never makes. The Bureau of Transportation Statistics also notes that airlines do not report the cause of late-arriving-aircraft delays, so weather at the city the plane is coming from is hidden inside another category ([BTS](https://www.bts.gov/topics/airlines-and-airports/understanding-reporting-causes-flight-delays-and-cancellations)).

Thresholds are local and graded, not universal. At Chicago O'Hare, a ceiling drop from 1,000 ft to 900 ft can cut arrivals from 100 to 92 an hour ([American Meteorological Society paper](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/81655.pdf)). Storms restrict an airport without rain overhead because they block arrival and departure routes ([Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/advanced-weather-forecasting-terminal-ops-plan-more-react-less)). Lightning within roughly 5 miles halts ramp work, though criteria vary by airline and airport ([Xweather](https://www.xweather.com/aviation/managing-lightning-risk-at-airports); [AMS survey](https://ams.confex.com/ams/95Annual/webprogram/Paper265940.html)). De-icing adds about 20 minutes for a small plane and 40 or more for a large jet ([NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2025-01-08/as-winter-storms-strike-airlines-scramble-to-de-ice-planes-and-keep-them-flying)). The research found no sourced crosswind, gust or snowfall-rate limits, so any wind or snow bands the site uses need their own sourcing.

What travelers lack is timely, plain information. **89% of US travelers** planning to fly are concerned about delays or cancellations, and among those significantly disrupted, half reported added stress and one in five missed the event they traveled for ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/19/89-travelers-disruptions-top-concern-paying-more-summer-travel/)). **57% of UK passengers** wanted more information during disruption ([UK CAA](https://www.caa.co.uk/publication/download/25553)), and 21% received no communication at all ([CMAC Group](https://www.cmacgroup.com/blog/75-of-uk-passengers-hit-by-flight-delays-and-cancellations-in-2025)). Most of these surveys come from firms selling insurance or flexibility products, so treat them as directional. No study was found that maps traveler questions to moments, but the evidence implies three: days before ("should I move my flight?"), the morning of ("should I still go?"), and mid-journey ("will I make my connection?").

Connections are where the gap is widest. Minimum connection times, about 30 minutes domestic and 60 international at large US airports, are feasibility floors under ideal conditions, not comfort margins ([AwardWallet](https://awardwallet.com/airlines/how-long-should-a-layover-be/); [Daily Drop](https://www.dailydrop.com/pages/minimum-connection-times)). No observed missed-connection rate by layover length was found from any regulator, airline or peer-reviewed source, so a site must derive its own estimate and label it as one. Hub exposure is well documented: O'Hare logged about 283,829 weather-caused arrival delays over ten years, the most of the 30 largest US airports ([Weather.com](https://weather.com/travel/news/2023-11-22-worst-weather-airports-us)), while San Francisco (40.32%) and LaGuardia (38.91%) lead on weather's share of holiday-season delays ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/airports-with-most-weather-delays-holidays-2026/)).

## Every incumbent holds one piece; nobody builds around the join

The market splits into three camps that barely overlap. Flight trackers treat weather as a layer or one delay reason among several. Weather products treat flights as a widget. Government pages are airport-level and coded. The table summarises what the notes support; "partial" means present but not joined to the itinerary or not driven by weather.

| Product | All itinerary airports' weather in one view | Plain-language risk verdict | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flighty | Partial: per-airport pages; connection rating is schedule-based | Yes, per flight and per connection | iOS only; key features paid |
| Weather Channel app | Partial: airport and flight widget | No combined verdict found | App |
| Google Flights | No | Per flight, only at 80%+ confidence | Free |
| FlightAware MiseryMap | Hub map plus radar, not itinerary-specific | No | Free, US only |
| Flightradar24 | No: paid map layers | No | Freemium |
| Windy | Only by building a route manually (Premium) | No | Freemium |
| aviationweather.gov | No: pilot products | No | Free |
| FAA NAS Status | No: airport list | No | Free, US only |
| TripIt Pro | No: alerts | Alert text, no probability | Paid |
| DelayGuard | No | Per flight, plus layover probabilities | Freemium, US domestic |

Flighty is the strongest competitor and the fastest-moving. It added machine-learning delay predictions in 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/06/flightys-popular-flight-tracking-app-can-now-predict-delays-using-machine-learning/)), a Connection Assistant that rates connections "relaxed, normal, tight, or at risk" ([Flighty help](https://flighty.com/help/connection-assistant)), and in March 2026 Airport Intelligence, which turns METAR, TAF and NOTAM data for about 14,000 airports into plain language ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/24/flightys-new-update-gives-you-real-time-alerts-about-airport-disturbances/)). Its weakness for this use case is structural: the connection rating rests on minimum connection time, and secondary coverage says the documentation does not confirm that weather intelligence feeds it ([Gadget Hacks](https://apple.gadgethacks.com/news/flighty-connection-assistant-explained-features-and-limitations/)). Its other weaknesses are commercial: no Android version, push notifications behind a subscription, and a reported price around $59.99 a year ([Trustpilot](https://jp.trustpilot.com/review/www.flighty.com); [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/flighty-a-pro-level-ios-app-for-frequent-travelers/); [Thrifty Traveler](https://thriftytraveler.com/news/travel/flighty-airport-intelligence/)). Reported prices conflict across sources and need re-verification.

The rest leave larger holes. MiseryMap overlays radar on US hubs but is situational awareness, not a forecast for a specific flight ([Your Mileage May Vary](https://yourmileagemayvary.com/2026/07/14/flightaware-misery-map-flight-delays/)). Flightradar24's weather is a tiered paid map layer ([Flightradar24](https://www.flightradar24.com/blog/inside-flightradar24/welcoming-weather-to-flightradar24/)). Windy's Route Planner is a Premium feature for self-defined waypoints, with nothing found that looks up a commercial flight or interprets delay ([Windy Community](https://community.windy.com/topic/23857/big-changes-same-price-premium-gets-an-upgrade)). Google Flights flags a delay only when at least 80% confident, which means near departure ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2018/01/31/google-flights-delays-budget/)). aviationweather.gov frames itself as a general-aviation briefing tool ([AWC help](https://aviationweather.gov/help)), and even frequent flyers ask for help decoding the FAA status table ([FlyerTalk](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/travelbuzz/297790-national-airspace-system-status.html)). Travelers are currently told to do the join by hand: "if you are connecting, check all three airports" ([EvvyTools](https://evvytools.com/tools/everyday-calculators/flight-delay-checker/)).

Seven gaps follow, in descending order of defensibility. The **itinerary join**: forecasts at each airport aligned to the traveler's own local times. A **weather-driven connection verdict** that combines hub weather with layover length. **Lead time** of several days that sharpens toward departure, where only KnowDelay claims a three-day horizon ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/travel/knowdelay-com-predicts-flight-problems-3-days-advance-flna1C9870958)). **Translation** of aviation weather into traveler language. **Free, web-first, cross-platform access**. **International coverage**, since the niche predictors are US-only ([DelayGuard](https://www.delayguard.ai/)). And **decision support**: which leg is the weak link, and whether a longer layover helps.

Two risks deserve a plain statement. Skift judged KnowDelay in 2012 to be probably "a feature" rather than a product ([Skift](https://skift.com/2012/11/14/knowdelay-reroutes-airline-passengers-around-weather-problems-3-days-in-advance)), and Flighty could close the main gap by wiring its weather feature into its connection rating. A crop of small 2026 sites repackaging free FAA and NOAA feeds shows the commodity layer is already crowded ([FlightQueue](https://flightqueue.com/tools/flight-delay-predictor); [FlightCheck](https://flightcheck.live/); [AirportDelay](https://airportdelay.com/features/airport-delays-map)). Differentiation has to come from the join and the multi-day outlook, not from mirroring FAA status. No independent accuracy test was found for any delay predictor, so honest uncertainty framing is a differentiator, not a weakness.

## Only two feeds work keyless in a browser; the rest need a proxy

The data picture is narrower than older tutorials suggest. The table combines documented terms with first-hand header checks run on 15 November 2026 against each endpoint.

| Source | What it gives | Key | Browser-callable (CORS) | Main constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) | Global hourly forecast, up to 16 days; geocoding | No | Yes, verified | Free tier is non-commercial, under 10,000 calls/day |
| [api.weather.gov](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api) | US alerts by point | No | Yes, verified | US only; do not set custom headers |
| [aviationweather.gov](https://aviationweather.gov/data/api/) | Observed METAR, TAF | No | No, verified | 100 requests/minute; needs proxy |
| [FAA NAS Status](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/api/airport-status-information) | US ground stops, delay programs (XML) | No | No, verified | US only; needs proxy |
| [OpenSky](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openskynetwork/opensky-api/master/docs/free/rest.rst) | Live aircraft positions | No (anonymous) | No, verified | 400 credits/day; no schedules |
| [adsb.lol](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adsblol/api/main/README.md) | Live positions by callsign | No, for now | No, verified | Feeder-only key announced |
| [adsb.fi](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adsbfi/opendata/main/README.md) | Live positions by callsign | No | Not tested | Non-commercial, 1 request/second |
| [adsbdb](https://github.com/mrjackwills/adsbdb) | Typical route for a callsign | No | Not tested | Static route knowledge, not a schedule |
| [RainViewer](https://rainviewer.com/api/transition-faq.html) | Past radar tiles | No | Not tested | Nowcast removed January 2026; low zoom |
| [OurAirports](https://ourairports.com/data/) | Airports, runways | No | Static file | Public domain |
| [mwgg/Airports](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mwgg/Airports/master/README.md) | Airports with IANA time zones | No | Yes, verified | MIT; 9 MB, bundle a trimmed copy |
| [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org) | Vector basemap tiles | No | Not tested | No SLA; commercial use allowed |

**Open-Meteo is the backbone and also the main legal risk.** It is keyless, CORS-open and global, with up to 16 days of forecast blended from ECMWF, GFS/HRRR, ICON and other models ([Open-Meteo docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)). Its free tier is limited to non-commercial use and under 10,000 calls a day; commercial use requires a paid plan on a keyed host ([Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms)). The live check returned HTTP 429 from a shared IP, so the per-IP quota is real. A site with ads or affiliate links needs the paid plan, and therefore a proxy to hide the key. The exact hourly variable names (visibility, gusts, precipitation probability, CAPE) were not confirmed from the docs in this research and need checking; these are model values, not a substitute for an observed METAR.

**Observed aviation weather and FAA status need a proxy.** Both aviationweather.gov and FAA NAS Status returned good data with no CORS header in the live check, matching an earlier developer report ([weather-gov/api discussion](https://github.com/weather-gov/api/discussions/739)). A single small serverless function that fetches and caches METAR, TAF and the FAA XML unlocks both. That breaks a strict "no backend" rule, but it is the cheapest upgrade with the largest payoff: real observed ceilings and visibility, plus live US ground stops.

**No keyless flight-number lookup with schedule or status exists.** Every schedule or status API found requires a key ([aviationstack](https://www.aviationstack.com/); [Geekflare](https://geekflare.com/guides/flight-data-api/)). OpenSky's airport arrivals are a previous-day batch, not a schedule ([OpenSky docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openskynetwork/opensky-api/master/docs/free/rest.rst)). The cheapest keyed options, on conflicting secondary pricing, are FlightAware AeroAPI's personal tier with up to $5 a month of free usage and AeroDataBox's small free tier ([SkyLink](https://skylinkapi.com/blog/free-flight-tracking-apis-2026/); [Thunderbit](https://thunderbit.com/blog/best-flight-api-with-free-tiers)); AeroAPI's personal tier is licensed for personal and academic use only. The honest keyless design is therefore a user-entered itinerary, with deep links out to the airline or a tracker for live status. OpenFlights route data is a June 2014 snapshot and must not be presented as current ([OpenFlights](https://openflights.org/data)).

**Map defaults have changed.** CARTO basemaps have required an API key since 25 September 2026, so common keyless Leaflet snippets now show a watermark ([CARTO FAQ](https://docs.carto.com/faqs/carto-basemaps)). OpenStreetMap's own tile server is for low-traffic use with attribution and a valid Referer ([OSMF tile policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/)). OpenFreeMap is the one option found that is keyless, uncapped and open to commercial use ([OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org)). RainViewer still serves past radar tiles for small-scale use, but nowcast frames ended on 1 January 2026 and its pages disagree on whether the free zoom cap is 7 or 10 ([RainViewer transition FAQ](https://rainviewer.com/api/transition-faq.html); [Weather Maps API](https://rainviewer.com/api/weather-maps-api.html)). A map is optional for this product; the verdict is not.

## Verdict first, a number beside every icon, three labelled levels

The display evidence is strongest on uncertainty. In the Met Office's mass-participation Weather Game, people shown a precipitation probability decided better than those without it, and far better than those shown only a weather symbol; extra uncertainty information did not confuse them ([Winton Centre](https://wintoncentre.maths.cam.ac.uk/news/new-publication-information-about-certainty-weather-forecast-helped-decision-making/)). The classic misreading of "30% chance of rain" stems from an unstated reference class, not from weak numeracy ([Gigerenzer et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/210832792.pdf)). Verbal terms such as "likely" are interpreted with wide variability ([Teigen](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9660216/)). Three rules follow: never ship an icon without a number, state what each probability refers to ("40% chance of any rain between 14:00 and 15:00 at Zurich Airport"), and pair every word with a figure.

Risk levels need redundancy and a stated reason. The Met Office's colour warnings collapse likelihood and impact into one colour, and in a 550-person experiment, amber warnings for unlikely but severe events drew the lowest trust and intention to act ([Taylor et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/203003/)). WCAG requires that colour never be the only carrier of meaning, and that status graphics hold 3:1 contrast ([W3C 1.4.1](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html); [W3C 1.4.11](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)). NHS Scotland's design system keeps red-amber-green but demands a visible label beside each data point and defined thresholds ([Turas](https://www.turasdesignsystem.nhs.scot/design/data-visualisation/status-palette/)). The recommended chip therefore carries a word, a distinct shape, a colour that survives greyscale, and a one-line reason that separates likelihood from impact: "unlikely, but would cause long delays". Three levels is a judgement call drawn from the yellow-versus-amber confusion; no study found prescribes an optimal number. Continuous values such as wind should use a perceptually uniform ramp, since rainbow and red-green maps distort data and fail colour-blind readers ([Crameri et al.](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1287014)).

Proven weather patterns transfer directly. Apple Weather's range bars sit on a shared track so position and length compare days at a glance ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/how-to/iphone-weather-app-bar)); the same device compares airports on a trip, though it needs a legend because explainers exist for it. yr.no's designers knew very few people understood meteograms, found users thought maps held too much information, and now keep the dense table behind a "Details" tab ([DOGA](https://doga.no/en/tools/inclusive-design/cases/yr.no); [Yr help](https://hjelp.yr.no/hc/en-us/articles/360020303414-FAQ-Transition-to-new-website)). Some yr.no users read the text forecast to validate the table ([ECMWF presentation](https://ecmwf.net/sites/default/files/elibrary/2015/13327-how-users-drive-us-making-complex-understandable.pdf)), so a sentence summary that agrees with the chart builds trust and doubles as the accessible alternative.

For time, product convention is the only guide; no formal research on multi-zone itinerary timelines was found. Show each airport event in that airport's local time with the zone labelled, and give elapsed durations explicitly ([InventiveHQ](https://inventivehq.com/blog/best-format-displaying-times-internationally)). Interviewees in one small case study did not understand "+1" beside an arrival time ([Medium case study](https://medium.com/geekculture/a-ux-case-study-improving-google-flights-4b3b6438601d)), so write the date out. The layout that follows is a vertical itinerary: one card per airport with its local time, date and risk chip, a weather strip covering the hours around the traveler's arrival and departure there, and the flight and layover durations between cards.

Navigation should be nearly absent. The landing page is the trip entry form plus one or two example trips, which follows Nielsen Norman Group's advice to use empty states to teach the feature and to distinguish loading, no-data and error states ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)). Shareable URLs that encode the trip, a persistent unit toggle and mobile-first layout are standard practice that this research did not source.

Put together, the page has three layers that serve four audiences. The top layer is one sentence: "Moderate weather risk at your Chicago layover: thunderstorms likely 15:00-17:00." That serves the anxious occasional flyer and the person doing an airport pickup. The middle layer is the per-airport cards with reasons, which serves the frequent flyer weighing a rebooking. The bottom layer is hourly data and, once a proxy exists, raw METAR, TAF and FAA advisories, which satisfies enthusiasts and lets anyone audit the verdict. These segments are working hypotheses; no published segmentation of this audience was found.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the product from "flight tracker with weather" to "weather verdict with an itinerary attached". The join is unserved; the tracking is not, and tracking is exactly the part free data cannot supply. That inversion is a strength. A site that asks for three airports and two times needs no flight API, works worldwide on Open-Meteo, and answers the days-ahead question that same-day trackers and FAA mirrors cannot.

Three decisions determine whether it holds up. First, licensing: decide early whether the site is non-commercial, because Open-Meteo's free tier and most ADS-B feeds forbid commercial use, and a commercial site needs a key and a proxy from day one. Second, the proxy: one cached function for METAR, TAF and FAA status is the difference between a model-only outlook and a credible same-day picture. Third, the scoring: with no public data linking forecast weather or layover length to missed connections, the thresholds are the site's own estimates. Publishing them, labelling the output as weather risk, and showing source and update time beside each verdict is what separates this from the crowd of FAA mirrors, and it is the hardest part for a rival to copy by wiring two existing features together.
